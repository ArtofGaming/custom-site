# Who I Thought I Was
When I was little I envied organized people a lot. My stuff was always messy and I lost stuff a lot. I was the kinda kid who a lot of people saw potential in but I never really felt like I could live up to it. While others could be super social or study constantly, I had been "learning" Japanese since I had been in elementary school without much progress to speak of. I always had ambitious goals for myself but often fell short. I kinda became known for it by those who were more than acquaintences. I thought I would never be organized and that only "Type A" people could do that (shiny perfect people) and that as an artist I was destined to always be messy, losing things, and that there was no use in trying to change it. It was how I was wired. Except that obviously wasn't working.

# Interlude
I believe it was on the suggestion of my therapist that I tried following a routine. We had tried a schedule at first, which did not work at all. I hate being held to tight timetables and prefer something where if needed, I can push times to work on something longer if I feel like it or swap things around on the fly. But I can form routines! For example, my breakfast works the same everyday. I eat the same thing, and recently I've been making myself a matcha latte as well. I usually drink the matcha while cooking my breakfast so I usually have juice when eating. It's not quite the same with the other meals but there is some routine to that as well. Meals made by me require a protein, a vegetable, and a starch/grain and are made in batches so I have multiple days of meals from them. But I was also starting to achieve goals and deadlines I had set for myself. Sometimes I did (and ocassionally still do) shoot too high, but I felt way better about myself afterwards.

# What Works For Me
For deadlines, a few things help me out:
1. Not everything needs a deadline.
While the "if you're not able to live with absolutely everything scheduled to the minute, then you just can't do any of it" all-or-nothing mindset is not helpful here or in most of life tbh. Some things can wait 'til you've gotten around to them or get slotted in when you need a boost. 

2. Work from an end/due/release date and go backwards
For example, in game jams you know exactly when the game has to be done. Then you know you need time to make the page which takes maybe an hour if you want it to look pretty without any css since you need screenshots and to choose tags. And before that the game should be tested which can take a few hours or one to two days. And before that, you need to have the game playable from start screen to end credits...etc.

3. Or start by estimating length of time for the tasks needed for your Minimum Viable Product
For any project, you should figure out what the lowest acceptable output looks like. For an art challenge can you do a sketch? Does the character have to be a complex one (humanoid, with heavy armor,etc) or can you do a little doodle? Based on whatever you decide your MVP, how long will the steps take and what are they? What steps are required to do those things? Depending on your experience on whatever you're doing you might want to multiply the amount of time you estimate by 1.5 or even 3 if you're doing a lot of things you're unfamiliar with. Also estimating time it'll take for tasks is easier if you just stick to days as your unit of measurement. Anything that takes 10 hours or less is one day, 32 hours is 4 days since I don't do fractional days.

4. Account for time off in your estimate
You should be giving yourself time off for projects without a super near deadline. If the project takes a week or more, you should account for break time. Optimally that's at least a day off during the weekend but it could also be a few hours off more often. 

For routines, the main thing is to remember that things can be based something other than time. A routine can happen due to a more loose tracking of time like breaking things up into morning, afternoon, evening, and night. Or you can do stuff after doing something else like cleaning up after a meal. I run my dishwasher right before I go to bed on nights that it's full, for example!
