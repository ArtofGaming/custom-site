# Tips for those just starting out
## Don't let your dreams be dreams!
*Anyone* can make a game. And I do mean that. I was 12 or 13 when I made my first one. I was not a particularly good RPGMaker user either. My game had a ginormous bug, one so bad that I had to manually quit the game and go into the editor to transport the player to the final boss. I think people think that when I say anyone can make a game, I'm saying anyone can make GTA6. No. When I say you can make a game, a game can be as simple as pressing a button and getting a reward. Hell, that's a whole genre, and incremental games are hot right now. Make a game, even and especially if it's terrible and buggy.
## Your engine doesn't matter
Except for a few specific scenarios (making a game for a niche or retro console for example) you can probably make your game in any engine. The main things you want to know are, can your engine do 2D/3D, and what language the engine uses. Also to be clear, you will be coding. Visual scripting (blueprints in Unreal, Scratch blocks, etc.) is programming. Scripting (putting the script into a game) is close enough. Don't spend too much time worrying over your engine.
## If you make your dream game, be smart about it
So, I'm not going to say not to make your dream game. Honestly, it's only now as someone in their 20's that I'm willing to start small and do a "boring" project to learn the basics of new things. If I had started making games "the right way" I wouldn't have made games until much later haha. So, I will just say approach it in a decent way. I saw a youtuber who was making their dream game... by making smaller games. They'd isolate a system in the dream game and make a small experience centering that.
## Solo devs don't dev alone
I am primarily a solo dev, but I do not dev *alone*. By that I mean that although I'm planning on doing the programming, art, character design, etc. myself, I have friends to support me. I'm friends with other devs that I talk to regularly, I'm in communities about game dev both online and in person. This also means that you don't have to do *everything* yourself. You can use an engine, there are people who make assets (both free and paid) for game devs to use from fonts to 3D models! You're not lesser if you use them.

# Tips for after your first game
## Share it
So the first thing you should do is celebrate! But you should also share your game with others. You can post it on itch or just show it to someone you trust. Don't be disappointed if they don't "get" your game though. Finding your audience will come later.
## Marketing is not the enemy
Marketing is just talking about your game. You are getting more people to play the game you made for people to play. It is not stalking twitter or whatever social media you love to hate. It can happen completely offline as well.

# Tips for After A Few Games
## Start looking into stuff you were scared of
Look into stuff you were intimidated by before or stuff you were warned not to do when you were newer to dev. It's tempting to just keep doing the same stuff you've been doing but now is the time to expand your horizons. Try a new engine! Try a new discipline! Learning can keep things interesting as you continue to develop.
## Document your journey
You don't have to become a social media star or anything. You don't even have to share this with anyone, but keeping a log of what you did, where you're going with your game, and so on will be so helpful. It helps a lot with motivation and organization even if you don't share it. If you do share it, you can use it for marketing as well!
