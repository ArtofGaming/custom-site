<dl>
<dt>1.) Introduce yourself! What kind of art do you make?</dt>
<dd>Hi I'm Cam! I do all sorts of art: 3d, 2d, digital, watercolors, music, and games as well.</dd>

<dt>2.) How long have you been making art?</dt>
<dd>Uh, it's been a really long time. I got more into improving and learning around late elementary school though.</dd>

<dt>3.) How many hours do you spend for each piece?</dt>
<dd>That honestly depends on what it is. 3d models take a few days but sketches and doodles can take less than two hours.</dd>

<dt>4.) Do you prefer taking your time or do you like to quickly move on to the next thing?</dt>
<dd>Quickly move on. While I do have some pieces I've been saving where I do want to take my time, I am very susceptible to losing interest in a piece or project if too much time passes since I've last worked on it. Keeping projects short means balancing all the other ones is easier.</dd>

<dt>5.) When do you decide a piece is finished?</dt>
<dd>There's a quote I really like that goes, "Art is never finished, only abandoned". I think that's how I've been feeling for a while. I get this feeling of just wanting a project to be over. The fun challenging problem solving bit is over and as soon as it's presentable, I let myself be free of it, even if it doesn't live up to my expectations. It's probably not the best, but it stops me from sitting on stuff forever and since admitting this to myself, I've felt a lot better.</dd>

<dt>6.) What part of the creation process do you enjoy the most?</dt>
<dd>I like the brainstorming process a lot. For visual stuff, sketching is my favorite part but only once I've got a concept down. For 3D, rigging is my favorite part, followed closely by doing the initial modelling to get the silohuette. My favorite part of writing is the actual drafting.</dd>

<dt>7.) What part of the creation process do you dislike the most?</dt>
<dd>I hate the polishing phase. I'm usually through with the fun part and a little tired of the project by then. Also taking photos/scanning/figuring out the render for 3d models also sucks.</dd>

<dt>8.) Do you have a designated workstation or do you just work wherever?</dt>
<dd>Kinda. I have a desk that I use for all my computer stuff and a desk I use for analog stuff. I also have a dedicated music area, although I have to rearrange it so I have more space to extend my bow fully. Despite that though I love curling up in my armchair and sketching or writing.</dd>

<dt>9.) Do you monetize you work or is it purely a hobby?</dt>
<dd>So I am hoping to monetize soon! The scope testing project may end up being my first purely commercial game. (I say "purely commercial" to mean a game where pay isn't optional or Pay What You Want (PWYW) like some of my games already are.) I think it would be cool to make digital zines and sell those or sell a comic, but those are much farther off.</dd>

<dt>10.) Where do you share your work? On social media or a personal websites? Do you keep to yourself and close friends?</dt>
<dd>I post art on my bluesky sometimes and I post games on my itchio when they're finished. I don't show off WIP stuff as often though, which I want to do more. I also want to eventually post my art on this site since it's a favorite page of mine on others' sites. But that's for later haha.</dd>

<dt>11.) What's an aspect of your art you enjoy?</dt>
<dd>That I can do so many different styles!</dd>

<dt>12.) What are things you would like to improve on?</dt>
<dd>There's a lot: drawing animals and anthros, getting faster, but honestly I think the thing that needs to improve most is just the frequency of how much I create. I can go quite a while between works and I don't like that since it means I have to spend time relearning how to do things.</dd>

<dt>13.) Are you self-taught or had formal education?</dt>
<dd>I had art classes in college! It's where I got to understand 3d and where I learned 3d animation.</dd>

<dt>14.) Do you research about old techniques or stick to newer ones?</dt>
<dd>I research old methods sometimes. I got a Loomis book from Barnes and Nobles and I swear it just improved my art so quickly by drawing along a little! Newer techniques are fun too but tbh what matters most in what techniques I look at is what creates art I like.</dd>

<dt>15.) What do you value more, experimentation or mastery?</dt>
<dd>Experimentation. I very much would like to learn how to create in many different ways. Mastery is good to strive towards but I wouldn't be interested in it if getting it meant I was restricted in style, medium, etc.</dd>

<dt>16.) Do you practice drawing from the arm? What other art techniques to you practice?</dt>
<dd>Not on purpose. Every once in a while I catch myself drawing from my arm.</dd>

<dt>17.) How frequently do you do studies?</dt>
<dd>I rarely do them. I really should. Although I did do gesture studies yesterday morning so progress!</dd>

<dt>18.) Do you understand color theory? How'd you learn or do you want to?</dt>
<dd>No, not really. I have a huge problem with contrast in particular. I might make a point to practice soon.</dd>

<dt>19.) Favorite color(s)?</dt>
<dd>Pink, green, and blue.</dd>

<dt>20.) Are there colors you avoid? Do you think ugly colors exists?</dt>
<dd>I don't avoid colors knowingly, sometimes they just don't vibe. Although whether it's that they don't vibe with the piece or that they don't vibe with my mood the world may never know. Colors being ugly is mostly a case of them not gelling with the colors around them for me though.</dd>

<dt>21.) Can you do perspective?</dt>
<dd>Certain ones sure. Extreme ones no.</dd>

<dt>22.) Do you like doing lineart? How do you go about it?</dt>
<dd>I used to hate doing lineart but now it doesn't bother me as much. I think what I hated wasn't actually lineart but the feeling that I had to replicate something perfectly only to lose the essence of what I was copying. I tend not to line in my art know though.</dd>

<dt>23.) Do you have multiple unfinished projects? Will you ever get to finishing them?</dt>
<dd>Oh for sure. I made a blog post about some of the ones from this year so far. Some of them I won't be able to dedicate enough time to in order to get done in a resonable time :(. I do try to go through old ideas and play with them just in case they can now be finished or if it was put down due to something other than scope.</dd>

<dt>24.) How do you start a new piece? Do you have a plan or just figure it along the way?</dt>
<dd>If it's an actual piece, I usually have some sort of concept in my head. Then I do a mix of translating what's in my head and adapting it to include better ideas and things I'm actually able to draw.</dd>

<dt>25.) How do you handle art block?</dt>
<dd>I don't usually have art block. Honestly, the reason why I don't create as much as I want to probably has more to do with me putting off actually creating.</dd>

<dt>26.) Do you listen to music or watch something while working?</dt>
<dd>That or some type of youtube video or I'm on voice call. Music is what I can work with longest though. Sometimes hearing someone talk is just too much noise and that builds to put me in a bad mood.</dd>

<dt>27.) When are you most motivated to create? In the morning or evening? Rainy or sunny?</dt>
<dd>I'm honestly not sure. When I'm socializing and eating well mostly. If there's any effect on my creation due to time of day, I dip in productivity around midday I think. And I don't think rainy weather affects my creativity much but I can work for a while if it's sunny and I'm outside (and it's not a crazy temp outside).</dd>

<dt>28.) Are your sketchbooks messy or aesthetically pleasing?</dt>
<dd>Neither? I don't usually have stuff scribbled out or anything I'd consider messy. Also, I have yet to see a non pleasing sketchbook. There's something so satisfying and pleasant about seeing someone's works laid out, but I don't try to make my sketchbook pretty.</dd>

<dt>29.) Do you like showing sketchbooks to others?</dt>
<dd>I'm mostly indifferent. I'll show if I need feedback or am just excited about what I drew.</dd>

<dt>30.) Do you have any art friends? IRL or online?</dt>
<dd>Mostly online, although I got a friend to start drawing so remember that you can also convert a friend to an art friend!</dd>

<dt>31.) Do you collaborate frequently with your friends? Is there something you'd like to work on together?</dt>

<dd>Yes, but a lot of them weren't exactly pretty. I did a really cool present for my dad that combined a low poly 3d render and digital art.</dd>

<dt>55.) Do you seek out for criticism? How do you handle feedback?</dt>

<dt>56.) Are you harsh on your own work? Or do you love your work despite all it's flaws?</dt>
<dd>I can be. I want to learn and get better so I worry about stagnation. I always feel that every piece can be improved even if only slightly. This used to be way more toxic though and I'd be incapable of accepting positive feedback at all because I was so convinced I couldn't produce something that good.</dd>

<dt>57.) Do you get compliments frequently? How do feel about them?</dt>
<dd>Uh, I guess. I generally don't pay attention much, unless it's on something I was struggling with, then they're the best thing I've ever heard.</dd>

<dt>58.) How do feel about the rise of generative AI? Do you use it for getting ideas or references?</dt>
<dd>I'm tired of it. It was pitched as a way to increase human potential but it hasn't done that. It's made people dumber, lazier and more conviced of their ineptitude and therefore need to use it. I don't use it purposely for ideas or reference although some stuff does slip through. I see it as pointless, a human could already do that. If a reference isn't perfect (which they shouldn't have to be anyway) then photobash it until it is perfect. A human already drew/made/took a picture of what you need to use, why bother with the AI?</dd>

<dt>59.) How do feel about tracing? What about tracing 3d models to streamline art process?</dt>
<dd>I was a young artist when the internet was crucifying others for tracing and it went from "don't pass this off as your piece" to "if you EVER trace you should die!". Everytime I talk to a pro artist, they talk about how they trace and the right way to do it. Trace that hand reference. Use that silohuette to make you face side profile. Seriously, this is an "issue" that mostly came from misunderstanding a situation where the issue was theft but got passed along as tracing being the problem.</dd>

<dt>85.) How do feel about censorship in art? Where do you draw the line for inappropriate art?</dt>
<dd>I'm gonna be for real, I think we should censor art less. I have seen works I find reprehensible but understand that when we start removing or banning people for making bad things, what's considered a bad thing slowly shifts. That being said, this is how I feel about governments and other large entities. Individuals and smaller groups should be able to protect themselves and I think things should be properly tagged to allow for that.</dd>

<dt>86.) Do you censor your own art? Do you feel like you leave out details for it be socially accepted?</dt>
